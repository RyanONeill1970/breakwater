# Breakwater: implementation notes

## Simulation contract

The core in `Scripts/Core` is ordinary .NET 10 C# with `System.Numerics.Vector2`; X/Y correspond to world X/Z. It does not call Godot. `Battle` steps it in `_PhysicsProcess`; `BattleView` renders that state. HUD commands reach `Battle` through `IBattleCommands` and feed the same simulation methods as keyboard orders. The opt-in Godot QA modes live in `QaDriver`, which is only added to the scene when a QA switch is passed; they exercise rendering integration and actual viewport input dispatch.

Every tuning number is a named constant next to the code that uses it: capture rules on `CaptureSite`, force structure and map extent on `Mission`, per-kind performance in `VehicleSpec`, and movement, combat and AI tuning at the top of the matching `Simulation` partial. Change it there and the HUD text follows.

Saves are written through private DTOs in `SaveGame`, not the live `Vehicle` objects. Adding a runtime field to `Vehicle` therefore doesn't change the file format. Only ids, kind, position, heading, health, ammunition, reload, fire discipline, AI tactic and queued orders persist; routes are replanned on load. Files from before this change, which also stored routes, speeds and status text, still load and those extra fields are ignored.

All values are game balance choices, not historical vehicle specifications or recovered original-game rules. Main tanks have greater range, damage and armor than scouts; scouts move and aim faster. All factions use the same collision and damage calculations.

## Movement

Navigation uses A* over a two-meter grid. Rectangular building/ridge footprints are expanded for a 2.1-meter vehicle radius. Route smoothing only removes corners when the entire hull can pass. Invalid destinations snap to nearby open ground; disconnected destinations reject the order. Up to eight destination orders per vehicle are supported. When a queued order has become unreachable by the time a vehicle gets to it, that order is dropped and the vehicle reports "No route to order" instead of waiting forever.

Hull rotation and acceleration constrain forward motion. Swept circle checks prevent vehicles and wrecks from overlapping, and swept expanded-rectangle checks stop entry into cover or the coast. A blocked vehicle waits briefly, then replans with other vehicles as temporary obstacles. It may back away from the planning margin while still respecting physical clearance. Narrow or crowded areas can require player-issued intermediate waypoints; this is not a full traffic/reservation system.

Direct driving controls the group's first surviving vehicle. G orders the other survivors to reform behind it; it does not tether the entire group to continuous manual movement.

## Combat

Turrets slew toward the nearest visible hostile. Buildings, ridges, other vehicles and wrecks occlude sight. A shot requires range, turret alignment, ammunition and reload readiness. Holding fire disables automatic fire but Space can request a shot from the lead vehicle.

Shells travel at 90 m/s. Each simulation step intersects the complete traveled segment with all cover and vehicle footprints and applies only the nearest impact. They can miss moving targets or strike friendly vehicles that cross the trajectory. No gravity, penetration, blast damage or destructible buildings are modeled. Frontal armor reduces damage; rear hits amplify it. Scouts have lighter protection.

## Reconnaissance and defenders

Both teams observe through the same terrain footprint checks: scouts have an 82-meter observation radius, other vehicles 60. Vehicles do not block reconnaissance; ballistic sight still includes vehicles and wrecks. Contacts are shared within a team and refresh every quarter second. Unobserved enemies disappear from the rendered battlefield, health bars and minimap. A hollow marker retains their last observed position for 30 seconds; it does not follow the hidden vehicle. Hidden combat effects and audio are suppressed, while damage to friendly units remains known.

The exploration grid has 46 × 46 four-meter cells. Current visibility clears its fog; previously explored cells remain dimmed. Terrain and objective locations are known from the outset. Exploration and both teams' remembered contacts survive saves. Visibility uses cell centers for the ground overlay and exact positions for vehicles, so there can be a small discrepancy at a fog boundary.

Some idle defenders patrol near their posts. A recent radio contact prompts an engagement or lateral approach; a stale report prompts investigation of its last known position. Defenders stop pursuing reports older than ten seconds and retreat when badly damaged or out of ammunition. They do not consult the current positions of hidden opponents to choose destinations.

## Enemy command

The garrisons no longer fight three separate battles. An enemy command (`Simulation.Command.cs`) reviews the island once a second and sends one force at a time:

- **Relief.** When the player starts capturing a site, up to two idle defenders from other garrisons drive to it.
- **Counter-attack.** Once a site has stayed secured for 20 seconds, up to three idle defenders go to retake it.
- **Reinforcements.** From 70 seconds in, a wave of three defenders lands on the north shore every 70 seconds: one wave on the first island, two on each of the others. If the player holds a site and no assault is under way, the wave counter-attacks it straight off the beach; otherwise it joins the weakest garrison.

Only healthy, armed defenders on guard or patrol are sent, and no garrison is drawn below two, so pulling defenders away weakens the sites they leave. After sending a force the command waits 40 seconds, and never sends a second while the first survives. An assaulting defender fights around its objective instead of its post. Enemy presence strips a secured site's status at once and drains its control at half speed; once control reaches zero with no friendly vehicle on it, the assault force becomes that site's new garrison.

Every order goes out as a radio call the player intercepts: a notice, a lower alarm tone, a large callout at the target or landing point, a minimap ping, and ENEMY INBOUND on the threatened site's labels until the force is destroyed or has retaken it. The forces themselves stay hidden by fog until spotted. All timings and sizes are constants at the top of the partial; wave counts, wave size and landing points live in `Mission`.

Damage produces a brief HUD hit ring, and badly damaged vehicles emit placeholder smoke. Combat samples use positional audio around the tactical focus or chase vehicle; command and contact cues remain non-positional. Muting stops current voices, and pausing freezes combat audio.

## Classic mode

Classic is a second rule set, chosen when a campaign starts (**Play Classic rules** on the briefing screen) and stored in the campaign as `Mode`. Modern play is unchanged: every Classic rule is gated on the campaign's mode or on the vehicle kind's `VehicleSpec.Classic`. The rules are loosely modelled on the early-90s island tank game that inspired Breakwater; the tuning, islands, layouts, text and art are Breakwater's own.

**Scale.** A reference battlefield is 6.4 km across and a Breakwater island 184 m, so `ClassicRules.Distance` divides reference metres by 32 and every range keeps its proportion to the island. Speeds use `ClassicRules.Speed`, one eighth of a world metre per second per km/h, so an M1 on a road is as fast as a Modern main tank.

**Forces.** The player fields the M1 Abrams, M2 Bradley, M113 and M901 ITV; the defenders are T-72s, T-62s, BMP-2s and BTR-60s, heavier on later islands. The loadouts map to Armor (four Abrams), Mixed (two Abrams, two ITVs) and Recon (two Bradleys, two M113s). Each kind has an armour value (M1 2000, T-72 1350, T-62 1000, Bradley 750, BMP 240, carriers 60) and a magazine of HEAT, sabot and missiles; every vehicle also has an unlimited machine gun.

**Weapons and damage.** Machine gun: power 1, 480 m. HEAT: 120, 1,900 m, 4.5 s reload. Sabot: 1,000, 2,500 m, 7.5 s. TOW: 2,100, 3,400 m, 18 s, and only at a halt. A hit takes `100 × power ÷ armour × roll` percent of health, with the roll between 0.3 and 1, and a vehicle below 1.5% health is destroyed. Vehicles choose ammunition by target (sabot, then missiles, then HEAT against tanks; HEAT first against fighting vehicles; HEAT or the machine gun against carriers) and loading a different type costs that type's reload. Targets are the biggest threat the team has spotted (armour over distance), not simply the nearest.

**Aim and cover.** A shot's bearing strays by a normal error of 0.011 rad, doubled if the shooter moves and again if the target moves, and multiplied by the cover of the target's ground. Shells still fly and collide as in Modern mode, and fall short at their weapon's range.

**Terrain.** `TerrainMap` lays a ground type on each four-metre reconnaissance cell from each island's hand-placed jungles, roads, tracks and river. Speed and cover:

| Ground | Speed | Enemy scatter | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Road | 100% | ×0.5 | |
| Track | 90% | ×0.5 | |
| Open | 60% | ×1 | |
| Jungle track | 60% | ×1 | concealed |
| Jungle edge | 50% | ×16 | the outermost cell ring of a jungle |
| Jungle fringe | 40% | ×8 | |
| Deep jungle | 25% | ×8 | concealed |
| River | 12.5% | ×0.5 | fordable anywhere; roads bridge it |

Routes minimise travel time: A* weights each step by the ground's slowness, and smoothing keeps a corner rather than trade a road for slower ground. A concealed vehicle is seen only from an eighth of the usual range until it fires; firing reveals it for 15 seconds.

**Gunner sight.** In Classic battles the view key cycles tactical, chase and the gunner sight: a first-person view from above the selected leader's turret, along the gun, at 1× or 6×. While it is up, `Simulation.GunnerId` names that vehicle and its turret stops tracking targets: the turn keys and the mouse (captured while the sight is up) traverse it at the turret's own slew rate, and the hull keeps its heading. Clicking or the fire key fires the loaded round straight along the gun (`FireGunner`), with the usual Classic scatter against whatever the gun is laid on; right-click loads the next ammunition type, paying its reload. The rangefinder (`RangeAlongGun`) reads the first hull or cover on the gun line, in reference metres, and names the vehicle if it is visible. If the leader is destroyed, the sight passes to the next vehicle in the group. The islands are flat, so there is no gun elevation. The sight is presentation state and is not saved.

**Group windows.** After the sight, the view key opens four windows, one per group (`GroupWindow`), in place of the side panels. Each has its own mode: the leader's gun sight (a `SubViewport` with its own camera in the battle's shared 3D world, so four sights cost four extra renders; windows not showing a sight stop rendering), the island map (a `TacticalMap` showing that group's orders, where right-click orders the group), or a status report of each vehicle's health, rounds and state. Clicking a window selects its group; in the selected group's sight window a click fires and right-click changes ammunition, and the turn keys lay that gun. Other groups' sights show their leaders' guns tracking targets by themselves. A window's ⤢ button shows its sight full screen, or the tactical map centred on the group. The view key goes on from the windows back to the tactical map.

**Support weapons** (`Simulation.Support.cs`). Abrams, Bradleys, T-72s and T-62s carry two smoke grenades; **C** fires one from each vehicle in the selected group that has any, throwing a screen about 200 m across ahead of its gun. Smoke blocks sight for both sides (reconnaissance checks each sight line against every screen) and clears after 40 seconds. The player has three artillery missions per battle: **B** readies one and each further press changes the shell (high explosive, cluster, smoke) or cancels, and the next right-click on the ground or a map fires it. Rounds start landing 12 seconds later, six of them scattered around the target, and hurt anything under them, friend or foe: damage is the shell's power over armour (high explosive 512 over a small radius, cluster 192 over a wider one), falling off with distance; smoke shells lay screens. The enemy has one mission per island number plus one, fired a minute apart at a player vehicle it has just reported once the fighting has started, and every enemy mission is announced with an alarm, an "INCOMING" callout and a ring where it will fall. **N** makes the selected leader drop a mine behind it (six per battle); mines arm after five seconds and wreck carriers and badly hurt tanks, whoever drives over them. Each Classic island also has a hidden enemy minefield across one road. The player's own mines are drawn in the world and on the map; enemy mines stay hidden until one goes off.

**Night.** The second island of a Classic campaign is fought at night (`Mission.Night`). The player's thermal sights see as far as by day; the enemy sees only 40% as far (`ClassicRules.NightEnemySight`), except that a vehicle that has fired in the last 15 seconds shows at full range. The scene gets a low moon and dim blue light, hulls glow faintly with engine heat, and every gun sight (full screen and in the group windows) looks through a green thermal filter: a camera environment that drains colour and maps brightness onto a green ramp.

**Gunnery comes first.** Who lays the gun changes where the shot goes (`ClassicRules`): the player's crews firing on their own scatter 2.5 times the base spread, the enemy's 1.5 times, and a gun the player aims through a sight 0.6 times, so taking the gunner's seat wins battles. Classic battles open in the group windows (three sights and Delta's map). Every Classic shell reports its result: `CombatEvent` carries the shooter and the damage, and a round that falls short or flies on reports a miss. The player's shots are called in the world, in the full-screen sight and in the group windows ("HIT T-62 38%", "KILL T-62 +$800k", "MISS", "FRIENDLY FIRE"), and hits on the player's own vehicles are called too.

**Fire discipline.** Classic battles open with both sides waiting (`Vehicle.Waiting`): nobody fires until an enemy it can see comes within 1.6 km (50 m, `ClassicRules.EngageRange`), or it or a comrade within 1 km has been hit. The player can open the fight sooner with weapons free (H) or the gun sight. Every garrison post is farther than that from the landing beach, so a force that lands and waits is never shot at; the first defenders of a garrison to open fire are reported on the radio.

**Sight and defenders.** Everyone observes to 2.4 km (75 m). Defenders re-think every 5 to 10 seconds. With an enemy reported within 2.4 km they stop and fight where they stand; otherwise they return to their posts. They never hunt, patrol or flank, and the enemy command sends no relief, counter-attacks or reinforcements.

## Classic economy

Classic campaigns count money, in units of $10,000 as the reference prices are (`Campaign.Money` shows them as dollars). A campaign starts with $55 million. Vehicles cost their reference prices (M1 $2.5M, Bradley $1.5M, ITV $1M, M113 $400k) for refits and replacements, and repairs cost in proportion to the price (a quarter of an M1's health is $250k). Winning an island pays $10 million, plus a bounty of each destroyed enemy vehicle's price (T-72 $1.5M, T-62 $800k, BTR $600k, BMP $200k), less $2 million for every civilian building the player's fire knocked down (`Simulation.Economy.cs`).

Buildings can be knocked down in Classic battles: gunfire and artillery damage them as if armoured like an M1 (2000), so a stray round cannot flatten one, and a destroyed building becomes rubble that no longer blocks movement, sight or fire. A building at least 22 m from every objective is civilian. Save version 7 keeps each building's health and the battle's bounties and fines.

## Classic zone campaign

A Classic campaign fights each island zone by zone (`ClassicCampaign`, `ZoneBattle`): several battles per island, tracked by `Campaign.Battle`, each with its own objective, capture sites, garrison and terms, on the island's ground and weather. Objectives (`ObjectiveKind`) are capturing every site, knocking down one building (a radar), destroying every enemy vehicle, holding a site until a set time, ambushing a convoy, or escorting one. An escort battle (`EscortConvoy`, `Simulation.Convoy.cs`) has friendly supply trucks outside the player's four groups (`Mission.ConvoyGroup`, `Vehicle.Convoy`): the player cannot command them; they wait at the beach, set off at a set time and drive their route to the end whatever is waiting, and enemy gunners rate them above any tank as targets. The battle is won when every surviving truck has parked at the destination and enough of them made it, and lost as soon as too few are left. Trucks do not count as the player's losses, and the battle is lost if the player's own vehicles are all destroyed even while trucks survive. Hold and ambush battles bring enemy columns (`EnemyColumn`, `Simulation.Columns.cs`) that arrive at set times and drive a route, fighting on the move: an assault column heads for the site and stays; a convoy that reaches the end of its route leaves the island. A hold battle is won when its site is secured once the time is up; an ambush when the convoy is spent and at least 60% of it was destroyed, and lost if more got away. Column vehicles keep their ids when saved (escapes leave gaps), and save version 9 counts the escapes. The islands run: Reef (two training battles, an ambush on the coast road, the stations), Breakwater at night (the radar, holding the seawall against two columns, the stations), Signal (clearing the jungle, escorting a supply convoy, the stations). The first island opens with two training battles, which have no enemy guns or minefields: a drive to an undefended relay that teaches roads, jungle and the river, and a gunnery range of old carriers that teaches the sight and artillery. Every island ends with the battle for all its stations, which is the island's original battle. Victory moves to the island's next battle, then the next island; the roster, damage and money carry over as between islands. The briefing shows a zone map of the island: zones won in blue, zones still held by the enemy in red, and the zone about to be fought outlined. Save version 8 stores `Campaign.Battle`; an older Classic save resumes at its island's final battle, which is the battle it was.

## Classic mission conditions

Some Classic battles add a term they must be won on (`BattleConditions`): Reef's station battle allows at most four vehicles lost, the Breakwater radar must be knocked down within ten minutes, and Signal's jungle must be cleared without destroying a civilian building. Breaking the term ends the battle as a defeat ("MISSION FAILED", with the reason), which retries like any other. Whether a term is broken is worked out from the battle's state (losses, elapsed time, fines), so saves need no new fields; a saved defeat with survivors is valid only when a term is broken. The objectives panel shows how the battle stands against its term.

## Retro look

**Retro look** in the settings (either mode) redraws the 3D view as an early-90s home computer would: `RetroFilter` puts a full-screen rect under the HUD whose shader (`Shaders/Retro.gdshader`) samples the screen at 320 pixels across (keeping the window's shape) with square pixels, reduces each channel to six steps (216 colours) and adds a 2 × 2 ordered dither. The HUD stays sharp. The group windows' sights render at a quarter of their size with nearest-neighbour scaling. It is a preference, saved with the settings and missing from older settings files, where it defaults off.

**Retro sound** resynthesises every cue as 8-bit samples at 8,363 Hz and plays at most four at once (two combat, two radio), like a four-channel sound chip. Both modes also gain two cues for Classic support weapons: a deep blast for shells and mines, and a falling whistle when enemy artillery is announced.

## Mission and persistence

Each of three authored islands has three reversible capture sites. A friendly unit must be within 16 meters, with no enemy within 20 meters, to build control for 12 seconds. Enemy presence reverses progress at half speed. All three must be secured simultaneously; losing all friendlies causes defeat. Wrecks do not contest capture.

Stationary friendly vehicles south of Z=64, with no visible enemy in their engagement range, repair at 1.8 health/second and rearm at two rounds/second. Destroyed vehicles cannot be repaired.

The `Campaign` state holds the island index, sixteen stable roster slots, supplies, previous mission time and cumulative prior losses. `Simulation` clones that deployment state and creates only occupied friendly slots (IDs 0–15); defenders keep IDs 16–27, and reinforcements landed during the battle take IDs from 28. Combat changes vehicle state, not the deployment snapshot. Retrying therefore restores the same deployment force. Advancing after victory creates a new campaign state from survivors and grants 100 supplies; the final island cannot advance. Earlier snapshots are not mutated.

Main tanks cost 40 supplies and scouts 25. Group refits exchange the difference for living vehicles while preserving health. Empty slots can change their intended vehicle type without generating a refund. Repairs cost `ceil(missing health * 0.15)` per survivor. Replacements fill one empty slot at a time. Transactions that cannot be afforded make no change. Transit replenishes ammunition but does not repair damage. Battle statistics cover the successful campaign path; retries discard failed-attempt time and losses.

Checkpoint version 6 stores campaign state, mission phase, battlefield, defender tactic state, reconnaissance and the enemy command: its dispatch cooldown, waves landed, how long each site has been secured, landed reinforcements after the deployed vehicles, and each defender's objective and any post that differs from its deployment. Version 6 adds Classic support weapons (missions and mines left, the enemy's next fire mission, mines on the ground, smoke screens, missions in flight and each vehicle's smoke). Version 5 adds the campaign's mode and each Classic vehicle's rounds, loaded ammunition and reveal timer; older checkpoints load as Modern campaigns. Version 1 checkpoints migrate to a fresh island-one campaign; version 2 campaign checkpoints initialize reconnaissance from loaded positions; version 3 checkpoints start the enemy command fresh. Writes use a temporary file followed by replacement. Loads validate campaign data, positions, team identities, health, ammunition, capture values, outcomes and reconnaissance records, rebuild routes, and pause active combat. Briefings and results retain their phase. Saving during active combat is disabled while shells are in flight; terminal mission effects can be discarded because combat has ended. Settings, audio voices, transient effects, camera position and shot counts are not saved.

Three manual slots use `user://quicksave.json`, `quicksave-2.json` and `quicksave-3.json`; slot one preserves the existing save path. F7 cycles slots, F6 saves and F9 loads. A separate `campaign-autosave.json` records the briefing immediately before deployment and after a successful island transition, restored with F10. It is not a periodic combat autosave. QA and visual previews isolate automatic saves under `.godot/qa`.

## Preferences and input

`GameSettings` owns 26 keyboard actions installed into Godot's InputMap with physical key codes. Both continuous driving/panning and discrete commands use those actions. F1 and Escape provide fixed access to settings and cancellation, while Shift and mouse controls retain their original behavior. Rebinding an occupied key swaps assignments, avoiding duplicate or unbound commands. HUD shortcuts and briefing hints reflect the applied bindings.

`SettingsPanel` edits a clone. Apply validates and atomically writes version-1 `user://settings.json` before installing bindings and applying audio, fullscreen and camera preferences. Cancel leaves both runtime preferences and the file untouched; defaults are provisional until applied. Opening records the prior pause state, pauses simulation, blocks gameplay input and restores that state on close. Muting or selecting a save slot outside settings persists immediately. Campaign checkpoints do not include these preferences. Malformed, incompatible or invalid settings use defaults and show a notice; load itself does not rewrite the file.

Master gain multiplies separate combat and radio gain. Zero gain stops and suppresses that category's voices; the existing mute control stops all voices. Camera speed scales keyboard and middle-drag panning. Settings QA and all preview modes use separate preferences and saves under `.godot/qa`.

## Validation

The console tests cover routing, disconnected paths, blocked destinations, collision, projectile occlusion, armor, ammunition, pause, capture, resupply, save validation and a complete mission. Campaign tests cover all spawn layouts, logistics accounting, sparse rosters, legacy checkpoint migration, retries, and an entire three-island run with checkpoint restoration. Reconnaissance tests cover terrain occlusion, scout range, remembered contacts, exploration persistence, hidden event suppression and defender patrol/flanking/retreat. Support tests cover smoke blocking and clearing, artillery delay, damage and mission limits, mines arming and detonating, enemy fire missions with their warning, and each island's minefield; a save test round-trips them. Zone campaign tests check every battle is well formed (garrisons clear of cover and each other, sites clear, targets are military buildings, nothing won at the start), that victory walks the battles in order to the end, that destroy and clear battles are won by their objectives, that the first training battle is won by driving to the relay, and that older Classic saves resume at the right battle. Column tests cover a convoy arriving on time and the ambush won or failed by how much of it gets away, hold battles won only by holding the site when time is up, and saving column vehicles and escapes. Condition tests cover each battle's term, the loss limit (including saving the failure), the time limit and sparing the village. Economy tests cover dollar amounts and reference repair prices, a civilian building knocked down by gunfire opening the way and costing a fine, bounties and victory pay, and saving rubble. Classic tests cover night sight ranges and muzzle flashes, the gunner's manual traverse, hand firing, ammunition cycling and rangefinder, the damage formula, ammunition choice, reloading on a type change, missiles at a halt, jungle-edge cover spoiling aim, concealment and reveal, terrain speed, road-following routes, Classic forces and refits, defenders holding their posts, a Classic save round trip and a fought Classic island. Enemy command tests cover relief without stripping garrisons, one force at a time, a counter-attack retaking an undefended site, reinforcement landings in both modes, and saving landed reinforcements. The three-island run plays like a player who turns back to retake lost sites. Godot's `--smoke-test` additionally checks live vehicle positions after combat and camera changes, and lands a reinforcement wave so the view must build vehicles mid-battle. `--ui-test` sends real viewport events to test deployment, controls, minimap orders, save-slot isolation, autosave restoration and regroup. `--classic-test` switches rules from the briefing button, checks the jungle scenery was built, deploys, enters the gunner sight with the view key, changes ammunition and fires with the mouse, opens the group windows, fires smoke, lays a mine, readies artillery with its key and fires it by right-clicking the minimap, switches one to its map with the window's own button and orders its group by right-clicking that map, returns to the tactical view, fights, and reads the ammunition readout. `--campaign-test` drives refits, transitions, repair/replacement, checkpoint restoration, defeat/retry and final completion through the actual UI; it injects outcome states to isolate UI flow from the separate full-combat simulation test.

Visual QA uses the OpenGL compatibility renderer and engine-generated viewport screenshots of the briefing, tactical view and chase view. Sound samples are synthesized locally; no external art or audio is required.

`--settings-test` exercises the real settings controls and viewport keyboard dispatch: key swaps, reserved-key rejection, capture cancellation, applying and discarding drafts, restoring defaults, command suppression while open, prior pause restoration, continuous movement, discrete command remapping, audio gain and dynamic hints. It rejects malformed, duplicate, missing, reserved and out-of-range settings data. `--settings-reload-test` runs afterwards in a fresh process, loads its saved preferences and verifies remapped driving again. The settings panel is also rendered for visual inspection. Headless checks cannot assess audible quality or fullscreen presentation.

## Known limits and next work

- Three flat islands; ridges are impassable cover rather than traversable elevation.
- Procedural placeholder visuals, a coarse fog grid and synthesized positional combat audio that still needs listening tests.
- Local avoidance is intentionally conservative and may need intermediate orders in congested formations.
- No multiplayer, terrain deformation, or full original-game mission recreation. Campaign content is original to this prototype.
- Before expanding content: human playtesting, broader accessibility/controller support, profiling with larger forces, and extracting mission definitions into editable resources.
